\begin{abstract}
The rising demand for home-based rehabilitative care has highlighted critical gaps in the accessibility and affordability of professional physiotherapy services. While digital health interventions offer a promising alternative, many existing systems suffer from high latency, privacy concerns related to cloud-based processing, and the prohibitive cost of specialized wearable hardware. This paper presents PhysioCheck, a privacy-preserving Edge AI solution developed to facilitate real-time posture correction and exercise tracking via standard web interfaces. To address the computational demands of real-time pose estimation, the system integrates a high-performance backend using Rust and WebAssembly, enabling efficient client-side execution that safeguards user data by keeping all processing local without video transmission. The analytical core of PhysioCheck employs a Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network enhanced by an Attention mechanism to accurately assess spatiotemporal skeleton dynamics and exercise quality. A significant challenge in home-based tracking is joint occlusion caused by limited camera angles or environmental obstacles; to mitigate this, the system incorporates a kinematic imputation module to predict missing coordinates based on anatomical constraints. Experimental results demonstrate that this approach achieves targeted reductions in False Negative Rates (FNR) and maintains a sub-20ms network-independent inference latency required for immediate biofeedback. By providing a robust, non-wearable alternative for clinical musculoskeletal assessment, PhysioCheck improves therapy compliance and offers a cost-effective tool for managing conditions such as forward head posture and hip rotation abnormalities.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The global healthcare landscape is witnessing a paradigm shift towards digital health interventions, particularly in the realm of musculoskeletal (MSK) rehabilitation. As clinical resources face increasing pressure, remote monitoring and home-based exercise programs have emerged as vital solutions for maintaining patient compliance and improving long-term recovery outcomes. The necessity for accurate posture correction is well-documented in the treatment of various conditions, ranging from spastic cerebral palsy to chronic lower limb symptoms, where gait and alignment serve as primary indicators of physical health. Furthermore, the integration of real-time visual biofeedback has demonstrated significant potential in enhancing therapy adherence and corrective accuracy following major procedures, such as total hip arthroplasty. Consequently, the demand for accessible, non-instrumented systems that can provide clinical-grade feedback in a domestic setting has never been higher.

Despite the clear demand for these technologies, current implementations of AI-based posture correction often struggle with the dual challenges of computational latency and data security. Many existing frameworks rely heavily on cloud-based pose estimation, which necessitates the streaming of high-resolution video data to external servers for processing. This architectural dependency introduces significant communication delays, which can disrupt the real-time feedback loop essential for corrective exercise. Moreover, the transmission of video data raises substantial privacy concerns, as sensitive biometric information is exposed to potential vulnerabilities during transit or storage on third-party servers. While advanced spatiotemporal skeleton-based learning models have improved the quality of exercise assessment, their high computational overhead often remains a barrier for seamless deployment on standard consumer-grade devices.

To address these limitations, this research introduces the PhysioCheck framework, a high-performance, browser-based alternative for real-time motion analysis and exercise tracking. By leveraging Edge AI, PhysioCheck shifts the computational burden from centralized cloud servers to the user’s local device. This transition is facilitated by WebAssembly (Wasm) and modern pose estimation techniques, which allow complex deep learning models to execute at near-native speeds within a standard web browser without the need for specialized hardware or wearable sensors. Such a non-wearable approach not only lowers the barrier to entry for patients but also ensures that all video processing occurs locally, thereby inherently protecting user privacy by eliminating external video transmission.

This paper argues that the convergence of Edge AI and WebAssembly enables a low-latency, highly accurate exercise tracking environment that operates entirely client-side, ensuring robust user privacy without compromising the depth of musculoskeletal analysis. By utilizing a Bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network integrated with an Attention mechanism, the proposed framework can effectively capture the intricate spatiotemporal dependencies of human movement, providing feedback that rivals instrumented clinical assessments.

The objective of this study is to evaluate the technical feasibility and diagnostic accuracy of the PhysioCheck framework through a rigorous analysis of its performance metrics. Specifically, this research addresses two primary questions: First, to what extent does browser-based Edge AI execution reduce inference latency and improve frame rates compared to traditional cloud-based architectures? Second, how does the application of a BiLSTM-Attention model enhance the precision of exercise repetition counting and the detection of subtle posture deviations in diverse, uncontrolled home environments? Through these inquiries, the study seeks to establish a new benchmark for accessible, private, and responsive digital physiotherapy.

\section{Related Work}

The integration of computer vision into musculoskeletal health has facilitated a paradigm shift in how physical therapy and exercise monitoring are conducted. Modern research focuses on transforming traditional clinical assessments into automated, real-time feedback systems that can be utilized in non-clinical environments. This literature review evaluates the technological foundations of pose estimation, the shift toward deep learning architectures, the role of edge computing, and the remaining challenges in deploying these systems for medical-grade use.

\subsection{Evaluation of Pose Estimation Frameworks}
The foundation of real-time exercise tracking lies in pose estimation frameworks that can accurately map human joints from 2D or 3D imagery. Two primary frameworks dominate current research: MediaPipe Pose and OpenPose. MediaPipe, a Google-developed framework, utilizes a lightweight BlazePose architecture that is optimized for real-time performance on mobile devices and browsers. In contrast, OpenPose employs Part Affinity Fields (PAF) to achieve high-precision multi-person tracking but requires significant computational resources, often necessitating high-end GPUs. Recent studies suggest that while OpenPose offers superior accuracy for complex gait analysis, MediaPipe’s lower latency makes it more suitable for smart mirror applications and interactive exercise feedback.

\subsection{Transition to Deep Learning Architectures}
The field of Human Activity Recognition (HAR) has evolved from traditional machine learning models, which relied on manual feature engineering, to sophisticated Deep Learning (DL) architectures. Current research emphasizes spatiotemporal skeleton-based learning, which treats the human body as a graph of connected nodes evolving over time. By employing Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) units, these systems can assess the quality of an exercise by analyzing the temporal flow of movement rather than isolated frames. This transition allows for the detection of subtle deviations in form, such as internal hip rotation or forward head posture, which are critical for preventing injury.

\subsection{Edge Computing in Healthcare Applications}
The shift toward edge computing is driven by the need for low-latency feedback and data privacy in medical applications. Processing video data locally on a user's device—rather than in the cloud—minimizes the risk of data breaches and ensures that feedback is instantaneous, which is vital for real-time posture correction. Non-wearable systems that utilize edge-based posture score estimation allow users to receive visual biofeedback without the discomfort of sensors. Research indicates that real-time visual biofeedback significantly improves therapy compliance, particularly in post-surgical recovery scenarios such as hip arthroplasty.

\subsection{Kinematic Imputation and Data Integrity}
A persistent challenge in computer vision-based tracking is the issue of missing data caused by self-occlusion or environmental factors. Kinematic imputation techniques have emerged as a solution to estimate the position of hidden joints based on the movement of visible body parts. By leveraging anatomical constraints and historical motion data, these techniques maintain the integrity of the skeletal model even when the camera's line of sight is partially blocked.

\section{Methodology}

The methodology for this real-time posture correction system is designed to bridge the gap between high-performance computational requirements and the accessibility of web-based platforms. By integrating low-level systems programming with advanced deep learning frameworks, the proposed architecture facilitates instantaneous feedback for home-based rehabilitation and fitness tracking.

\subsection{System Architecture and Execution Environment}
To achieve near-native execution speeds within a browser environment, the system architecture employs a hybrid approach utilizing Rust and WebAssembly (Wasm). Traditional JavaScript-based applications often encounter performance bottlenecks during high-frequency data processing and complex mathematical transformations required for kinematic analysis. By offloading heavy computational tasks—such as coordinate normalization, skeletal vector calculations, and data serialization—to a Rust-based backend compiled to Wasm, the system minimizes latency.

\subsection{Pose Estimation and Coordinate Extraction}
The primary data acquisition layer utilizes MediaPipe Pose to extract 33 3D landmarks from a single RGB camera frame. To address self-occlusion, the methodology incorporates a kinematic imputation algorithm. This algorithm utilizes the hierarchical constraints of the human skeleton to estimate the position of occluded joints based on the coordinates of visible parent joints and historical temporal data.

\subsection{Temporal Feature Extraction and Exercise Classification}
The system utilizes a Bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network integrated with an Attention mechanism to process sequences of skeletal landmarks (input tensor shape: $30 \times 99$). The BiLSTM architecture captures temporal dependencies from both past and future frames within a sliding window. The Attention mechanism assigns varying weights to different joints depending on the exercise being performed. The final model is converted to a quantized format and executed on the client-side via TensorFlow.js.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Dataset \& Data Split}
The evaluation was performed on a Feasibility Evaluation Dataset comprising healthy volunteers performing correct and incorrect movement patterns. To prevent subject leakage, the data was divided using a strict subject-independent split, ensuring that no participant contributed samples to more than one partition (Train/Validation/Test). All participants provided informed consent for video recording and research use.

\subsection{Safety-Critical Positive Class Definition}
For form assessment, the safety-critical positive class is defined as the \textbf{Incorrect movement}. A false negative (FN) therefore corresponds to an unsafe movement being incorrectly classified as acceptable (safe). Threshold calibrations explicitly target minimizing this FNR to prioritize patient safety.

\subsection{Deployment Hardware/Software}
All local latency and performance measurements were conducted on consumer-grade hardware configurations representing standard home environments, utilizing standard web browsers with WebGL hardware acceleration enabled for TensorFlow.js and SIMD enabled for WebAssembly execution.

\section{Limitations}

While this study demonstrates the technical feasibility of browser-based Edge AI, it is not without limitations. Foremost, the evaluation was performed on healthy participants mimicking incorrect forms rather than patients with rehabilitation-related pathologies. Clinical validation against expert physiotherapist ground truth using actual post-operative patients is required to validate real-world efficacy. Additionally, frontal-view webcams struggle to capture subtle lateral anomalies (e.g., knee valgus), necessitating multi-angle validation in future work.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}

The PhysioCheck system addresses the critical gap in home-based rehabilitation by providing a scalable solution to the common barriers of poor exercise adherence and the lack of professional supervision. By transforming a standard camera into a sophisticated diagnostic tool, the system offers real-time corrective feedback. The technical contribution of this research lies in the successful integration of BiLSTM-Attention architectures with Edge AI via Rust WebAssembly and TensorFlow.js. This decentralized approach eliminates the latency issues and security risks associated with cloud-based computation. Future work will explore multi-modal sensor fusion and clinical validation trials to further establish the system's efficacy in real-world rehabilitative care.
